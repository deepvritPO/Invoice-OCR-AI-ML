    How pentagon Ludo works

    Classic Ludo is four arms of thirteen cells — fifty-two in all. This variant adds a fifth arm, so the shared ring is 5 × 13 = 65 cells and the board is a pentagon rather than a cross.

    • Leaving base. A token only leaves its base on a 6, landing on that player's start cell.
    • The lap. From its start cell a token walks all 65 ring cells. Sixty-four steps later it reaches its own arm tip, then turns inward into its private home column of five cells.
    • Exact roll home. The goal sits 70 steps from the start. You must roll the exact number to enter it; an overshoot is simply not a legal move.
    • Safe squares. Ten cells are safe — two per arm: every player's start cell, and the star cell eight steps further on. Nothing is ever captured on a safe square. Arm tips are not safe.
    • Capture. Landing on an unsafe ring cell holding opponents sends every opponent token on that cell back to its base — and, with the rule switched on, earns you another roll.
    • Blocks. Two or more of your own tokens on one ring cell form a block. Opponents may neither land on it nor pass through it; your own tokens pass freely. Home-column cells are never blocks.
    • Sixes. A 6 earns another roll — but a third six in a row forfeits the turn entirely, with no move played.
    • Finishing. Get every token to the centre. The first player home takes first place, and the rest keep playing for the remaining four places.
